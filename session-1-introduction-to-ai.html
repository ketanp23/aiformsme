02 — The four you'll actually meet

Four kinds of AI, four jobs they do for a small business.

You do not need the science. You need to recognise which kind of AI does which job — so you know what to reach for when a problem shows up on your shop floor or in your accounts book.

ML

Machine Learning

Learns patterns from your past

You give it history — past sales, past repairs, past orders — and it spots the pattern and predicts what comes next. It does not "create"; it forecasts and sorts.

For a spice-packaging unitFrom three years of sales, it flags that turmeric and garam masala spike 6 weeks before Diwali — so you produce ahead instead of running out.
GEN

Generative AI

Creates something new

Give it an instruction and it produces fresh work: a product description, a label design, a marketing photo, a festival greeting for customers. A tireless junior assistant for creative tasks.

For a steel-furniture maker"Write a 4-line WhatsApp catalogue caption for a foldable study table, ₹2,400, delivery across Nagpur." — done in seconds, ready to tweak.
LLM

Large Language Models (LLMs)

The assistant you talk to

The engine behind ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. You ask in plain Hindi, Marathi or English; it understands and answers. It can draft, summarise, translate, explain and reply.

For a fabrication workshopPaste a customer's rough requirement and ask it to turn it into a clean, itemised quotation with GST — a first draft in one minute.
AGT

Agentic AI

Does the task, not just the talk

The newest step. Instead of only answering, an "agent" carries out a series of steps for you — check, decide, act — using tools and information, with your permission.

For an FPO / agri-collectiveAn agent reads incoming buyer enquiries, checks available stock of tur dal, drafts a price reply, and lists which orders need your approval — you just review.

04 — The mindset shift

Normal software follows rules. AI learns from examples.

This is the single most useful idea in the session. Tally, a calculator, or your billing app follow fixed rules you set. AI is trained on thousands of examples and figures out the rule itself. That is why it can handle messy, human tasks.

Rule-based software

  • You must spell out every rule in advance.
  • Same input, same output, every single time.
  • Breaks the moment something unexpected arrives.

AI that learns

  • Learns the pattern from many past examples.
  • Handles wording and situations it has never seen.
  • Improves as it sees more of your real data.

The three kinds you'll use most each work differently underneath. You don't need the maths — just the feel for it. Try all three tabs: guess the next word, predict from data, and watch an agent work step by step.

You are the LLM. Complete the sentence.

An LLM is a very powerful guessing machine — it picks the most sensible next word, again and again. Build a sentence yourself.

Pick the word that sounds most natural. Notice how you are not "looking up" an answer — you are sensing what fits. That is exactly what an LLM does, at massive scale.

You are the ML model. Predict from the pattern.

A namkeen unit's Diwali-season sales, year by year. Machine Learning studies the past and predicts what comes next — no rules written, just the pattern.

Looking only at the trend — how many packs should you make for Diwali 2026?

Each year rises by roughly the same amount. Pick the prediction that follows the pattern.

Watch an agent work through the steps.

A chatbot would just reply. An agent plans and acts — checking, calculating, drafting — then pauses to hand the decision back to you.

07 — Discovery skill

Which kind of AI is this? Learn to name it on sight.

Once you can glance at any task and sense whether it's a job for an LLM, Machine Learning or an Agent, use-cases start jumping out at you across every function. Here's the whole rule in three questions — then test yourself.

LLM

Is it about words?

Reading, writing, replying, summarising, translating, explaining.

Signal words: draft, reply, write, summarise, translate, explain, read.

AI / ML

Is it about predicting from data?

Forecasting, scoring, sorting, detecting or recommending — from your past records.

Signal words: predict, forecast, score, sort, detect, spot, recommend.

Agentic

Is it a multi-step job with actions?

Carrying out a whole task across steps and tools — monitoring, chasing, coordinating.

Signal words: monitor, chase, coordinate, process, dispatch, reconcile.

10 — Business framework

Know what to hand to AI — and what to keep for yourself.

AI is not good at everything, and that's fine. The skill is knowing which jobs to give it and which to keep. Learn this line and you'll never be disappointed or caught out.

Hand it to AI

  • Language work — drafting, replying, rewriting, summarising, translating between Marathi, Hindi and English.
  • First drafts of anything — a quote, a caption, a letter, a plan. Blank-page problems vanish.
  • Finding patterns in lots of data — sorting, grouping, spotting trends in your past sales.
  • Tireless repetition — the same kind of task, a hundred times, without getting bored or sloppy.
  • Explaining things simply — a scheme, a rule, a new idea, in words you choose.

Keep it for yourself

  • Exact arithmetic — it can slip on precise sums. Recompute prices, GST and totals yourself.
  • The very latest facts — unless it can search, it may not know today's rate or rule. Verify.
  • Your private information — it only knows what you tell it; it can't see your books or stock on its own.
  • Final judgement & responsibility — the decision, and the accountability, stay with you.
  • Relationships & trust — the human touch with a customer or worker can't be outsourced.
