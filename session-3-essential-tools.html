03 — The prompt recipe

A good prompt has four parts: Role, Task, Context, Format.

The difference between a disappointing answer and a brilliant one is almost never the tool — it's how you ask. Give the AI these four things and it stops guessing. Remember it as R-T-C-F.

R

Role

Tell it who to be. This sets the tone and expertise.

"You are my sales assistant…"
T

Task

Say exactly what you want done, in one clear line.

"…write a WhatsApp offer message…"
C

Context

Give the details only you know — product, price, audience.

"…for 20% off masala packs this Diwali, for regular customers in Nagpur…"
F

Format

Say how the answer should look — length, language, style.

"…in Marathi, under 4 lines, warm and friendly."
THE FULL PROMPT, ASSEMBLED
You are my sales assistant. Write a WhatsApp offer message for 20% off masala packs this Diwali, for regular customers in Nagpur. Reply in Marathi, under 4 lines, warm and friendly.
04 — Framework: the 5 levels of prompting

R-T-C-F is level 2. Here's how to go further when you need to.

Most of your work only needs levels 1–2. But when an answer isn't good enough, climbing a level almost always fixes it. Tap each level to see the trick and an example.

1

Just ask

Type your request in plain words. Fine for simple things.
Good enough for quick, low-stakes questions. The weakness: the AI has to guess everything you didn't say.
"Write a thank-you message for a customer."
2

Add Role, Task, Context, Format

The R-T-C-F recipe. Your everyday default.
Tell it who to be, what to do, the details only you know, and how the answer should look. This alone fixes 80% of weak results.
"You are my sales assistant. Write a thank-you WhatsApp to a customer who bought 5kg of chikki, inviting them to reorder. Warm, 3 lines, Marathi."
3

Show an example

Give it a sample of the style you want it to copy.
Paste one message you've written before and say "write more like this". The AI matches your tone and format far better than any description.
"Here's a message I liked: [paste]. Write 3 more in the same style for these products."
4

Ask it to ask you first

Let the AI interview you before it answers.
For anything important, tell it to ask you questions first. It gathers exactly what it needs, and the final answer is sharper.
"Before writing my business plan summary, ask me any questions you need, one at a time."
5

Break it into steps & self-check

For big tasks, guide it step by step and have it review its own work.
Split a large job into stages, and finish with "now check your answer for mistakes". This catches errors before you do.
"First list the sections. Then draft each one. Finally, review the whole thing for anything unclear or wrong."

10 — Use it safely and well

A short list that keeps you out of trouble.

AI is powerful, not perfect. These habits protect your customers, your money and your reputation. They take two minutes to learn and save you real grief.

Do

  • Check every fact and figure. AI can sound sure and still be wrong — verify prices, GST, dates and legal points yourself.
  • Treat it as a first draft. You are always the final editor and the one responsible.
  • Write in your own language. Hindi and Marathi work fine — use whatever you think in.
  • Save your best prompts. Keep a note of what worked so you and your team can reuse it.
  • Start small and free. Prove value on low-stakes tasks before paying for anything.

Don't

  • Don't paste sensitive personal data. No Aadhaar, PAN, bank details or customer phone lists into public AI tools.
  • Don't share trade secrets. Keep secret recipes, formulas and pricing strategies out of it.
  • Don't send unchecked replies. Never forward an AI message to a customer without reading it first.
  • Don't trust numbers blindly. AI is weak at exact arithmetic — recompute anything that matters.
  • Don't let it decide alone. Use it to inform your judgement, never to replace it.
